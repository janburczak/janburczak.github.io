Research

Nonlinear partial differential equations, regularity versus singularity regimes, hydrodynamics.

My work concerns well-posedness of partial differential equations coming from the natural and life sciences — incompressible and compressible Navier–Stokes equations, non-Newtonian flows, transport and mixing, the related toy models, and chemotaxis. The recurring question is where the boundary between regularity and singularity actually lies, and what mechanism decides it. Two of the tools I use most, homogenisation and convex integration, come from opposite ends of the subject; several of my recent results come from making them talk to each other.

Anomalous dissipation and turbulent transport

with L. Székelyhidi Jr. and B. Wu

The zeroth law of turbulence asserts that dissipation survives in the vanishing-diffusivity limit. For scalars advected by a rough velocity field, we established this by a new connection between homogenisation and the geometric, convex-integration perspective on PDEs.

  • Anomalous dissipation and Euler flows — the weak zeroth law of scalar turbulence, with the velocity governed by an Euler flow. JEMS, to appear
  • Scalar anomalous dissipation and optimal regularity via iterated homogenization — the strong zeroth law, beyond the Yaglom threshold, for divergence-free velocity. arXiv:2604.13912, submitted

These results are discussed in an invited lecture at the 2026 ICM, and were the subject of my invited summer-school course at Sorbonne Université in 2025.

Pathological solutions in dissipative fluid models

Dissipation is often expected to rule out wild behaviour. In a series of papers I show that this expectation fails in natural dissipative settings — including regimes previously believed to be safely well-posed — by constructing non-unique or otherwise pathological solutions.

  • Non-uniqueness of power-law flows (with S. Modena, L. Székelyhidi Jr.) — ill-posedness driven by the quadratic nonlinearity, for small p. CMP 2021
  • Non-unique power-law flows with any p ≥ 2 (with S. Schiffer) — ill-posedness driven by quasilinearity, now for every p ≥ 2. submitted
  • Pathological solutions of Navier–Stokes equations on T² with gradients in Hardy spaces (with A. Hidalgo) — very weak solutions in 2d, with the full gradient controlled. JFA, to appear

Fractional Keller–Segel systems

with R. Granero-Belinchón

Chemotaxis models with fractional dissipation sit exactly on the regularity–blowup threshold, which makes them a sharp test case for critical and supercritical techniques.

  • Critical Keller–Segel meets Burgers on S¹: large-time smooth solutions — disproof of the finite-time blowup conjecture in the critical one-dimensional case. Nonlinearity 2016
  • Global solutions for a supercritical drift-diffusion equation — regularity in certain supercritical cases. Adv. Math. 2016
  • Related: suppression of blowup by a logistic source in the 2d fractional system. JDE 2017

Well-posedness beyond duality pairing

with M. Bulíček and S. Schwarzacher

For nonlinear systems under singular forcing, the natural energy pairing breaks down. We developed a well-posedness theory that operates outside it, in the steady and then in the evolutionary setting.

  • A unified theory for some non-Newtonian fluids under singular forcing. SIMA 2016
  • Well-posedness of nonlinear parabolic systems beyond duality. AIHP-C 2019
